Residential Home Inspections

Before becoming a home inspector, I worked as a realtor and saw firsthand how important reliable information is during a property transaction. After working with multiple inspectors on my own purchases and sales, I recognized the need for inspections that are both thorough and balanced.

My role is to identify defects, safety concerns, and system issues that most buyers would not know to look for — while keeping findings in proper perspective. Not every issue is a deal-breaker. An inspection should provide clarity, and possibly an improved negotiating position.

If the home is right for you, you should be able to move forward with confidence.

Commercial Building Inspections

Commercial property decisions carry significant financial responsibility. Just because you aren't buying a building, it doesn't mean you should skip the inspection. Many business leases require tenants to cover maintenance and repairs, which makes understanding the building's condition critical before signing an agreement.

A commercial inspection provides insight into structural components, major systems, and visible deficiencies so you can evaluate potential costs and negotiate from an informed position. Knowing what you are stepping into protects both your business and your investment.
